Is Online Poker Legal in the US? State Licenses vs. Offshore Access

The US has no single answer, because internet poker law is set state by state and two systems sit side by side.

State-regulated markets. Nevada, New Jersey, Delaware, Pennsylvania and Michigan have run licensed internet poker. Operators there hold a license from the state regulator, such as the New Jersey Division of Gaming Enforcement (NJDGE), the Pennsylvania Gaming Control Board (PGCB), the Michigan Gaming Control Board (MGCB) or the Nevada Gaming Control Board, and geolocation confirms that you're physically in the state when you play.

West Virginia and Connecticut authorize online gambling, but at our last check they had no active regulated poker market. Legislation changes, so check your state regulator's site for the current position, and note that the minimum age in these markets is generally 21.

The federal layer. No federal law makes it a crime for an individual to play internet poker. The Unlawful Internet Gambling Enforcement Act of 2006 targets payment processing for unlawful gambling businesses, which is why bank and card friction persists, and the Wire Act's application to non-sports gambling has been disputed in federal courts and Justice Department opinions.

Shared liquidity. The Multi-State Internet Gaming Agreement (MSIGA) lets member states pool poker players across state lines. Nevada, Delaware, New Jersey and Michigan have shared liquidity under it, and West Virginia has signed on, while Pennsylvania's participation is worth checking directly, as its status has been the subject of ongoing discussion.

Where can I play poker online for real money if my state has no license?

In most states, no local licensing system exists, and offshore networks accept players from many of those states. Accepting you doesn't make them authorized, since they hold no US license, and some states, such as Washington, explicitly prohibit online gambling.

Being able to sign up tells you nothing about whether it's legal where you are. Check your state's law, and the room's own excluded-states list, before you sign up.

Where licensed rooms win. When a payout goes wrong, a state regulator gives you somewhere meaningful to go. Banking works through ordinary channels and state self-exclusion registries are enforced, which for players inside those states is significant protection.

Where offshore rooms win. Liquidity is the main advantage, because a single-state pool can be thin, especially outside peak hours and in variants like PLO, while offshore networks draw from most of the country. They also lean harder on rakeback and loyalty value, which matters to volume players.

The risk side. An offshore room's license gives you less recourse, and its security standards are self-reported more often than independently confirmed. That doesn't mean every offshore room is unsafe.

It means the burden of checking falls on you, including the network's payout history, how it handles bots and collusion, and how long its license and ownership have been stable. No poker room, licensed or not, can be described as completely safe.

Who Licenses Offshore Poker Websites, and What That Means for You

Offshore rooms usually hold a license from a small-jurisdiction authority. These regimes differ a great deal in how strictly they supervise, how they handle player complaints and whether they publish their licensee registers.

  • Curaçao: Historically the most common license for offshore poker and casino brands. The jurisdiction has been reforming its framework toward direct licensing by a central authority, with stronger compliance expectations, so check whether a room holds a license under the current regime and appears on the public register.

  • Panama: Some long-running sportsbook-and-poker brands have operated under Panamanian licensing. Oversight focuses on licensing and compliance, and there's little visible player-dispute infrastructure.

  • Anjouan: An autonomous island of the Comoros that has become a popular license source for crypto gambling operators. It's relatively light-touch, so verifying a license on the official register is especially important.

  • Kahnawake: The Kahnawake Gaming Commission, based in the Mohawk Territory of Kahnawake in Quebec, has licensed online gambling since the late 1990s and runs a published complaints process.

  • Tobique: A more recent licensing body associated with the Tobique First Nation in New Brunswick. It has a shorter track record, so give it more scrutiny.

Domain ownership and network checks

A room's license should name a legal entity, and that entity should match the company named in the terms and conditions. If the footer license number doesn't resolve to an entry on the regulator's register, or the named company differs, treat that as a warning sign.

For network skins, the network operator often holds the security and game-integrity responsibilities, while the skin handles your account and cashier.

Fair play audits and dispute resolution

Some offshore rooms commission third-party RNG audits, while others publish nothing. If you have a dispute, the usual order is the room's support, then its formal complaints process, then the licensing authority, and you should expect far less enforcement power than a US state regulator has.

Domestic bodies like the NJDGE, PGCB and MGCB can fine operators, suspend licenses and order player refunds, which is why the best real money poker sites for residents of licensed states are the regulated ones. Offshore regulators' practical leverage over an operator is weaker.

Online Poker Bonus Terms, Rakeback and Loyalty Rewards Explained

A poker bonus works differently from a casino bonus. Casino bonuses are usually cleared by wagering a multiple of the bonus, while poker bonuses are usually released to you in small increments as you pay rake, so the headline figure is a ceiling you might reach, not money you receive on day one.

Sign-up match bonuses at the best poker sites

A match bonus pairs your first deposit with bonus money up to a cap: 100% up to 2,000 dollars at ACR, Black Chip and Ya Poker, 100% up to 1,000 dollars at BetOnline, and 100% up to 500 dollars at Bovada, while Jack Poker's 300% up to 2,000 dollars reaches its cap with a smaller deposit.

The material conditions are the release rate, the expiry window, eligible games and whether specific deposit methods are excluded. We didn't have full terms for each of these offers, so read them on the operator's site before you deposit.

Release rates and a worked example

Here's an illustrative example using assumed figures, not any operator's actual terms. Suppose a room releases 5 dollars of bonus for every 20 dollars of rake you generate, and you claim a 500-dollar bonus.

  • Rake needed to release 500 dollars - 500 ÷ 5 × 20 = 2,000 dollars in rake

  • Hours needed - if you generate about 25 dollars of rake per hour at 1/2 No Limit Hold'em (an assumption), that's 80 hours of play

  • Daily volume - with a 60-day expiry window (also an assumption), that's about 1.3 hours a day, every day

At those assumptions, the full 500 dollars is effectively a 25% rebate on 2,000 dollars of rake, which is a decent return. But if you only play 20 hours in that window, you release 125 dollars and the rest expires, because unreleased bonus funds are almost always forfeited at expiry and can't be withdrawn.

How rake is charged in cash games

Cash-game rake is a percentage of each pot, up to a maximum cap per hand that usually rises with stakes and table size. As an illustration, with 5% rake and a 3-dollar cap, a 40-dollar pot pays 2 dollars in rake, while a 100-dollar pot pays 3 dollars, not 5 dollars, because the cap applies.

Many rooms take no rake from hands that end preflop ("no flop, no drop").

Dealt rake vs. contributed rake

How a room credits rake to you affects your rewards. Under the dealt model, everyone dealt into a raked hand gets an equal share, so a 3-dollar rake at a six-handed table credits each player 50 cents, even if they folded preflop.

Under the contributed model, you're credited in proportion to the money you put in the pot, so if you put 50 dollars into a 100-dollar pot, you're credited 1.50 dollars of the 3 dollars. Contributed rake favors aggressive players, while dealt rake favors tighter players.

Tournament entry fees

Tournaments use a fixed fee instead of pot rake. A 100+10 event puts 100 dollars into the prize pool and charges 10 dollars as the room's fee, which is 10% of the buy-in that goes to the pool, so ten such entries cost 100 dollars in fees whatever your results.

Fees as a percentage often fall at higher buy-ins and rise at micro-stakes.

Free tournament tickets and freerolls

Some rooms add tournament tickets or freeroll access to welcome packages. Tickets often have their own expiry and can only be used in specific events, so treat them as a small extra, not core value.

Reload bonuses at the best online poker sites

Reloads are match bonuses on later deposits, usually with smaller caps and the same release mechanics. They're worth taking only if you were going to deposit and play the required volume anyway, and you should never deposit more than you planned just to unlock one.

Flat vs. tiered rakeback

Flat rakeback returns a fixed percentage of your rake, so as an illustration, 27% back on 300 dollars of monthly rake is 81 dollars. Tiered programs start lower and rise with volume, so a casual player might get 15% while a high-volume player reaches 40% or more.

Your effective rakeback is everything returned (rakeback, released bonus money, leaderboard prizes and ticket value) divided by the total rake you paid. That figure, not the headline percentage, is the fair way to compare these rooms for a volume player.

Withdrawals, KYC and Account Security When You Play Poker for Real Money

Every cash-out goes through two separate stages, and most delays happen in the first one. Operator approval is the room's internal review of your identity, bonus status and account activity, while payment settlement is the time the money takes to reach you after approval, which is minutes on a blockchain and days through banking channels.

When a room advertises "fast payouts," find out which of the two stages it's describing.

Identity verification (KYC) for US online poker players

Playing on an offshore network doesn't let you skip identity checks. Expect to provide government-issued photo ID, proof of address and sometimes proof of payment-method ownership before your first withdrawal, and occasionally again for large ones.

State-licensed rooms verify identity at sign-up, often including the last four digits of your Social Security number, plus geolocation each session. Complete verification early, before you need a cash-out, and make sure your account name and address exactly match your documents.

Withdrawal limits and common delays on online poker for money

Rooms set per-transaction, weekly or monthly withdrawal caps that differ by method. Typical causes of delay include:

  • Incomplete or mismatched documents

  • Withdrawing while a bonus is still active, which may forfeit unreleased funds

Multi-accounting and bot detection

Every reputable room bans holding more than one account, including across skins on the same network, and breaking that rule usually means account closure and confiscated balances. Networks also run behavioral checks for bots, real-time assistance software and collusion.

Account security on poker sites

Real Money Poker Games, Formats and Software Features

Most readers already know the games. What differs between rooms is which formats run reliably at your stakes and what the software lets you do.

Cash games from online Texas Holdem to PLO, PLO5 and Short Deck

No Limit Hold'em dominates every room on this list. Pot Limit Omaha is the second pillar, and liquidity there separates big networks from small ones, since WPN and PaiWangLuo usually run PLO at several stake levels, while smaller rooms may only spread it at micro and low stakes at peak hours.

Five-card PLO and Short Deck appear mainly on the larger networks. Mixed games such as limit Hold'em, Stud, Razz and rotations are a niche, and while ACR has offered them periodically, don't count on mixed games anywhere offshore at all hours.

If you're mostly after online Texas Holdem at micro to low stakes, any of the major networks will usually have tables running. Moving up in stakes is where network size starts to decide things.

Online poker tournaments with guarantees, PKOs and satellites

MTTs are where network size shows most clearly, because large guarantees need large fields, which is why ACR's schedule and CoinPoker's series stand out. Progressive knockout (PKO) events pay part of each buy-in as a bounty and are now standard across major rooms, and satellites let you win seats into bigger events for a fraction of the buy-in.

An overlay happens when entries fall short of the guarantee and the room covers the difference. That improves the value of each entry, but it's never certain in advance.

Fast-fold and Sit & Go online poker formats

Fast-fold poker moves you to a new table and hand as soon as you fold. Ignition and Bovada call theirs Zone Poker and WPN runs Blitz, and it's useful for volume and tends to tighten the field.

Standard Sit & Gos have become less common. Lottery-style Jackpot Sit & Gos are short three-handed hyper-turbos with a randomly drawn prize multiplier, common on WPN, with high variance and a prize-pool structure in which most entries win a small multiple or nothing, so budget for them like any other high-variance game.

Multi-tabling caps and auto-seating when you play poker online for money

Rooms cap how many tables you can play at once, and caps vary by format. Auto-seating, waitlists and "sit at all similar tables" features save time for grinders, while recreational players rarely notice them.

HUD compatibility on poker sites

Tracking tools like PokerTracker and DriveHUD read hand histories to display stats on opponents. WPN has historically allowed approved tools under a permitted-software policy, while anonymous-table rooms like Ignition and Bovada make real-time HUDs ineffective by design.

Native HUDs built into the client, where offered, give every player the same information. Check the current permitted-software list at each room, because violations can lead to closure.

Anonymous tables vs. screen names

Anonymous rooms protect recreational players from being tracked and targeted, while screen-name rooms suit players who want to build reads and study opponents. Neither is better in absolute terms, and which suits you depends on your role in the player pool.

Online casino poker vs. peer-to-peer poker

Some accounts also include house-banked games like video poker, Caribbean stud or Three Card Poker. You play these against the house, with a fixed edge, not against other players, and skill doesn't change that edge the way it can at a poker table.

They sit in the casino lobby, not the poker client, and bonus terms often treat them separately.

Responsible Online Poker With Limits, Self-Exclusion and Support

Poker involves skill, but skill doesn't remove the risk of losing money, and short-term variance is large, with many players losing over time. Treat poker as paid entertainment, set a budget you can afford to lose, and stop when you reach it.

Don't chase losses or move up in stakes to win money back.

Account limits

Look for deposit limits, loss limits, table stake limits, session timers and cooling-off periods in your account settings. State-licensed rooms are required to offer these tools, while at offshore rooms they vary, and where they're missing, ask support to apply limits or close the account.

Regulated states run self-exclusion registries, run by bodies like the NJDGE, PGCB and MGCB, that every licensed operator in the state must honor. Offshore rooms aren't connected to those registries and only offer their own account-level exclusion.

If you've self-excluded in your state, please don't use offshore or sweepstakes sites to get around it. Your exclusion is there for your protection, and the rooms on this page aren't a route around it.

US Online Poker Questions, Answered Briefly

Is online poker legal under federal law for US residents?
No federal law makes it a crime for an individual to play online poker. The UIGEA of 2006 targets payment processing for unlawful gambling businesses, not players. Legality is decided state by state. A handful of states license online poker. Others say nothing on the subject, and a few, such as Washington, explicitly prohibit online gambling. Check your own state's law before you play.
Are my funds and the games safe on offshore poker networks?
Nobody can promise that. Offshore rooms are not supervised by any US regulator, so there is no state-enforced fund segregation and no state body to hear your complaint. Long-running networks with consistent payout records lower the risk but don't remove it. Keep only your active bankroll on the site and cash out the rest regularly.
How is sweepstakes poker different from a real-money poker room?
Sweepstakes platforms such as Stake.us use two currencies. Gold Coins are for play only. Sweeps-style coins can be redeemed for prizes once their playthrough conditions are met. You don't wager cash directly, and the legal model relies on a no-purchase-necessary entry route. A growing number of states restrict or ban these platforms. Expect smaller poker liquidity and different redemption rules than at a cash poker room.
Why do US credit card deposits fail at poker cashiers?
Many US card issuers block transactions coded as offshore gambling, and the UIGEA puts pressure on banks to screen them. A decline usually comes from your bank, not the poker site. Some issuers also treat a successful deposit as a cash advance, with fees and interest. Crypto and P2P transfers are the usual alternatives.
Are crypto withdrawals fast and reliable for US players?
Once a room approves a crypto cash-out, the blockchain transfer usually settles in minutes to a few hours. The slow part tends to be internal review, especially the first withdrawal and any that trigger identity checks. Being reliable also means sending to the correct network and address, because crypto transfers can't be reversed.
Can I use a HUD or tracking software on US-facing poker networks?
It depends on the network. Winning Poker Network rooms have generally permitted approved tracking tools under a published software policy. Ignition and Bovada run anonymous tables, which makes HUDs pointless during play. Room rules aren't laws, but breaking them can get your account closed and your balance confiscated. Read each room's permitted-software list before installing anything.
How does rake work in cash games versus tournaments?
In cash games the room takes a percentage of each pot, up to a fixed cap per hand. Many rooms skip the rake on hands that end preflop. In tournaments the fee is built into the buy-in: a $100+$10 event sends $100 to the prize pool and $10 to the room. Rakeback and loyalty schemes then return part of what you paid.
What should I do if an online poker payout is delayed?
Check the cashier status first. Then make sure your verification documents are complete and match your account details. After that, contact support in writing and keep the ticket number. If the delay runs well past the room's stated timeframe, escalate through the license holder's complaint process. Don't open a second account or reverse a deposit to force things, because both can lead to the account being closed.
